# Quality rebuild

The first implementation was a prototype, not an acceptable visual or gameplay baseline. This rebuild replaces its rendering and world/gameplay code while preserving account saves, release dates, and validated story milestones.

## Acceptance criteria

- **Home:** a stylized rendered cemetery, restrained interface, coherent art, parallax/fog/light motion, no flat icon-card wall.
- **Last Ride:** actual perspective 3D railway; physical cart, curved track scenery, authored obstacle combinations, jump/duck/lane/brake decisions, readable warnings, speed progression, animated station arrival and ghost handwriting inside the rendered scene.
- **Delivery:** scrolling 3D town, substantially larger map, textured road/building detail, collision solids, substepped vehicle physics, grip/handbrake, traffic, damage, short-distance delivery interaction, navigation/minimap.
- **Grave Robber:** 5120×4480 connected cemetery with 11 rooms/courts, randomized endless caches and supplies, fuel-scaled visibility and ghost-stopping beam. Two starting zombies; pressure increases with digging and low fuel. Shadows removed. Full floor plan, explored tiles, room labels, cache markers, visible enemies and routed navigation. Twelve-page illustrated guide with shovel practice; T reopens it. Continuous wall-aware light mask, near-black shuttered lantern, articulated player/mob animation, charging Gravebound. Crawlers/stone throwing removed. Ghost beam resistance, faster fuel drain with a gentler high-fuel curve, zero-fuel survival, six treasure types and 22% supply drops. Population escalates with active run time as well as finds/fuel.
- **Mystery:** rotating the starting lamp, finding the fixed shadow, tracing the hatch, entering the chamber, taking the lantern, Keeper awakening and escape remain in the cemetery game. The ghost writes during its terminal cutscene. No substitute prose page for those events.
- **Verification:** test collision boundaries, route logic, frame-rate-independent physics, story gates and saves; inspect the new scenes in-browser, not only syntax checks.

## Technology

Three.js, pinned and vendored locally with its license, supplies WebGL rendering for the two 3D games. Grave Robber uses Canvas 2D with a shared, code-authored pixel atlas and world/camera coordinates. All live scenes use the palette in `engine/pixel-art.js` and nearest-filtered material textures. Generated realistic art is retained as unused experiments; it is not loaded by the games. Firebase configuration and trusted progression are retained.
